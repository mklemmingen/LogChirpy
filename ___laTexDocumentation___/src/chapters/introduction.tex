\section{Introduction}

Citizen-science platforms such as eBird~\cite{Sullivan2009} aggregate hundreds of
millions of bird observations, yet data quality depends on observers accurately
identifying species in the field.
Mobile apps with automated recognition lower this barrier; BirdNET~\cite{BirdNET2021}
demonstrated macro-averaged AUC\,$>$\,0.99 for audio-based classification across
thousands of species on consumer hardware.
Image-based classifiers (e.g.\ MobileNetV2~\cite{Sandler2018}) are complementary
but are rarely integrated with audio in a fully offline mobile context.

LogChirpy addresses three gaps: (1)~multi-modal on-device identification combining
BirdNET audio with a MobileNetV2 image classifier, requiring no cloud inference;
(2)~a multilingual offline encyclopedia of 33,241 species from the Clements 2024
checklist~\cite{Clements2024} with six-language translations and 9,331 Wikimedia
reference images; and (3)~a sequential pipeline service that eliminates the
file-lock race conditions arising when audio recording and camera capture run
concurrently on Android.
Sections~\ref{sec:arch}--\ref{sec:conc} describe the architecture,
an independent image-classifier benchmark, and limitations.

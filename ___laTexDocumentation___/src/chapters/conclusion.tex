\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conc}

\subsection{Contributions}

LogChirpy's primary contribution is integration rather than novel modelling:
BirdNET audio and MobileNetV2 image classification are delivered in a single
offline-capable mobile application alongside a 33,241-species multilingual
encyclopedia — the largest we are aware of in a mobile-embedded open-source
ornithological tool.
The sequential pipeline service is a replicable solution to a documented
Android concurrency problem.
The BirDex corpus (33,241 species × 6 languages × 9,331 images) and the
benchmark script constitute independently reusable artefacts.

\subsection{Limitations}

\textbf{Image coverage:} 400 of 33,241 species (1.2\,\%); audio remains the
primary modality.
\textbf{No original models:} both classifiers are pre-trained third-party
weights~\cite{BirdNET2021,Sandler2018,BirdClassifier2023}.
\textbf{Benchmark shift:} editorial Wikimedia images $\neq$ field conditions.
\textbf{Translations:} automated; not validated by domain-expert native speakers.
\textbf{Scope:} 12-week student project; no peer review or long-term field trial.

\subsection{Conclusion}

Multi-modal, fully offline bird identification is feasible on commodity Android
hardware within a student project timeframe.
Future work should expand image classifier coverage beyond 400 species, conduct
a field-condition accuracy study, and integrate observation upload with GBIF or
eBird to produce research-grade records.

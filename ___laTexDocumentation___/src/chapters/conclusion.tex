\chapter{Discussion and Conclusion}

\section{Scientific Value and Scope}

LogChirpy's primary scientific contribution is not a new ML model but an
\emph{integration} contribution: it delivers BirdNET audio identification and
a MobileNetV2 image classifier in a single offline-capable mobile application,
paired with the largest publicly available mobile-embedded ornithological
encyclopedia (33,241 species, 6 languages, 9,331 reference images).

The sequential pipeline architecture solves a concrete engineering problem
(audio/camera race conditions on Android) in a way that is replicable and
documentable; the fix is committed and described in this paper.

The BirDex database and its 9,331 Wikimedia image corpus constitute a reusable
open dataset.
The benchmark script (\texttt{dev/benchmark\_image\_classifier.py}) provides
a reproducible evaluation baseline for future mobile bird image classifiers.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{description}
  \item[Image coverage.] MobileNetV2 covers 400 of 33,241 species (1.2\,\%).
        For the remaining 98.8\,\%, image classification returns the
        nearest trained class, which may be meaningless.
        Audio (BirdNET, 6,522 spp., $\approx$65\,\% of recognised species)
        is the primary identification modality.

  \item[No original model training.] Both classifiers are pre-trained third-party
        models~\cite{BirdNET2021,Sandler2018,BirdClassifier2023}.
        LogChirpy integrates and delivers these models; it does not improve them.

  \item[Benchmark distribution shift.] Wikimedia reference images are
        editorial quality; field-photo accuracy will be substantially lower.

  \item[Translation quality.] Automated mass translation of 33,241 species
        names across six languages was spot-checked by family but not
        formally validated by native-speaker ornithologists.

  \item[Scope.] This is a 12-week student project; it has not undergone
        peer review, formal usability testing, or long-term field evaluation.
\end{description}

\section{Conclusion}

LogChirpy demonstrates that multi-modal, offline-capable bird identification
is feasible on a commodity Android device within a student project timeframe.
The unified sequential pipeline, the BirDex encyclopedia, and the reproducible
image-classifier benchmark are the three concrete artefacts offered to the
research community.
Future work should (1) expand image classifier coverage beyond 400 species,
(2) conduct a field-condition accuracy study, and (3) integrate the app
with a GBIF or eBird submission pipeline to convert observations into
research-grade records.
